Mule rings move money fast
and keep none of it.

Victims pay a collector, the collector splits it across layers, a cash-out account sends it on. Ringwatch looks for accounts that forward almost everything they receive within hours, in a short burst, then joins them into rings. It runs in your browser. The payments are made up, and the rings are planted so it can be scored.

    Method

    1. Match. For each account, every outgoing payment is matched first-in-first-out to money that arrived in the previous 48 hours. The matched share of total inflow is the forward ratio.
    2. Burst. Count the days that carry 80% of the account's money movement. Mules finish in a few days. Merchants and normal accounts spread over the month.
    3. Flag. Inflow of at least 300, forward ratio of 0.6 or more, 3 days or fewer.
    4. Join. Flagged accounts linked by a direct payment form a ring. The senders into a ring are shown as likely victims.

    Limits

    • The data is synthetic and I wrote both the generator and the detector. The easy set is a sanity check, not evidence. The hard set adds long delays, big fees, camouflage spending and more merchants, and the detector misses a third of the mules there.
    • Real systems also use device, identity, and account-age signals that a payment graph does not have.
    • Thresholds were set once on the easy set and not changed for the hard set. Moving them trades recall for false alarms.
    • Flagged does not mean guilty. A real flag needs a human to review it.
